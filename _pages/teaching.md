## WS 2026/27 - Online Learning and Algorithmic Pricing

I teach a seminar on online learning and algorithmic pricing, focused on history-dependent payoff fields. Students investigate how memory and feedback affect equilibria, local and global stability, and the emergence of oscillations or chaos in repeated pricing dynamics. The course combines mathematical analysis, Python experiments, and formal verification in Lean 4, with an emphasis on developing and checking AI-assisted proof arguments.

## SS 2026 - DRL-AAS

I teach a lab course on pricing agents in oligopoly competition, where students study how LLM-based pricing systems behave in repeated Bertrand markets. The course combines economic theory, demand modeling, algorithmic baselines, and reproducible experiments to evaluate pricing behavior, welfare, regret, and market outcomes.
