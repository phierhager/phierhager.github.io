## Thesis supervision

I am open to supervising Bachelor's and Master's theses related to optimization, dynamical systems, learning theory, game theory, stochastic algorithms, and formalization of mathematical methods.

Bachelor's theses are usually suitable for focused literature reviews, numerical experiments, or simplified models. Master's theses can be more research-oriented and may involve theoretical analysis, algorithm development, formalization, or more substantial computational work.

If you are interested in a topic, please email me with a short description of your background, the topic you are interested in, and any relevant coursework or project experience.

## Open thesis proposals

### Non-Euclidean Geometry and Stability of Player-Wise Mirror Learning in Games

**Level:** Master thesis  
**Duration:** 6 months  
**Type:** Research thesis with theorem, counterexample, and worked examples  
**Area:** mirror descent, learning in games, Banach geometry, variational inequalities  
**Status:** open

This project studies which Hilbert-space stability mechanisms for player-wise learning in games survive under non-Hilbert Banach geometry, and which fail. The thesis combines geometry-dependent stability analysis, explicit failure modes of Euclidean proof arguments, and a positive relative-monotonicity principle for mirror learning in games.

### AI-Assisted Formalization of Finite Learning in Games in Lean 4

**Level:** Master thesis  
**Duration:** 6 months  
**Type:** Library-building thesis with a fully verified core theorem  
**Area:** Lean 4, finite games, regret, Nash equilibrium, coarse correlated equilibrium  
**Status:** open

This project builds a reusable Lean 4 library for the finite-game foundations of learning in games. The central target is a verified theorem showing that low external regret implies an approximate coarse correlated equilibrium for the empirical distribution of play.

### AI-Assisted Formalization of Bregman Geometry and Mirror Learning in Lean 4

**Level:** Master thesis  
**Duration:** 6 months  
**Type:** Library-building thesis with a fully verified core theorem  
**Area:** Lean 4, Bregman geometry, mirror descent, variational inequalities  
**Status:** open

This project builds a reusable Lean 4 library for Bregman divergences, variational inequalities, monotone operators, and mirror-learning foundations. The central target is a formal mirror-descent regret or Lyapunov theorem based on the Bregman three-point identity and telescoping Bregman divergences.
