## The problem

How should a classical particle move when its path reaches a junction? On a metric graph, each edge is an interval with its own Hamiltonian dynamics. At a branching vertex, energy conservation determines the outgoing speed but leaves the choice of outgoing edge unresolved.

This project supplies the missing rule at each vertex and studies whether the resulting motion preserves the fundamental conservation properties of Hamiltonian dynamics.

## What this work shows

- Energy-preserving vertex scattering maps prescribe how incoming states continue through a junction.
- Away from the finitely many vertex energy levels, the edge dynamics and scattering rules combine into a global measurable flow.
- This flow preserves both energy and the quotient Liouville measure, without requiring a smooth symplectic structure on the quotient phase space.

The result connects familiar motion along individual edges with a consistent deterministic dynamics on the whole graph.
