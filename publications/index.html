    Last-Iterate Convergence of Gradient Learning in Bayesian First-Price Auctions Beyond Monotonicity
    Martin Bichler, Philip Hierhager, and Jan Hoehener
    2026
    Soon on arXiv
    Abs

    We prove last-iterate convergence of symmetric payoff-gradient learning in Bayesian first-price auctions with a continuum of private values, although monotonicity and the Minty condition fail. We use an exactly equivalent procurement representation for the analysis. The key is an exact identity: subtracting the equilibrium turns the bid dynamics into a damped transport equation for the equilibrium error, and a comparison principle for this equation replaces monotonicity. The identity holds exactly when the winner’s surplus is affine in its own price. For every number of bidders \(n\) and every value distribution with a density bounded away from zero and infinity, the dynamics have a unique global solution from every initial bid function with bounded positive slopes, and the sup-norm error decays exponentially for \(n=2\) and as \(t^{-1/(n-2)}\) for \(n\ge3\); both orders are sharp. Nonnegative bids and no overbidding fix the lowest-value bid at zero; without this constraint, it is learned for \(n=2\) and frozen for \(n\ge3\). A fixed-step grid algorithm with gradient errors \(\eta=O(1/N)\) on \(N\) cells converges geometrically to an \(O(\eta)\) neighbourhood of equilibrium in the uniform duopoly; this noise scale is necessary up to a factor of two. All numbered results are formally verified in Lean 4.

    How language model agents reshape price competition
    Martin Bichler, Philip Hierhager, Kassian Köck, and 1 more author
    2026
    Abs arXiv PDF

    Repeated Bertrand competition is a stringent benchmark for algorithmic pricing: in homogeneous-goods markets, standard learning procedures typically select the stage-game Nash outcome with prices near marginal cost. We show that LLM pricing agents can escape this benchmark, depending on deployment decisions. LLMs outperform standard online learners through faster adaptation, but this advantage largely disappears against full-information learners. An extracted surrogate algorithm illustrates how LLMs in specific deployments profit from regularly undercutting slower learners. Agents that post one price every period from raw market histories that include rival prices can sustain supracompetitive regimes in self-play. Agents that periodically emit probability distributions from summarized histories of their own profits instead move toward the competitive region in self-play. Markets mixing these interfaces can generate dynamic regimes in which LLMs establish a high-price anchor and regularly undercut it to capture demand. These regimes can keep both competitors’ prices above marginal cost. Deployment choices shape these outcomes, shifting the focus from stationary equilibria to dynamic pricing regimes.

    Liouville-Preserving Hamiltonian Scattering on Finite Metric Graphs
    Philip Hierhager
    2026
    Abs arXiv PDF

    A metric graph with a mechanical Hamiltonian on each edge does not, by itself, define a deterministic classical motion through a branching vertex: conservation of energy fixes only the outgoing speed, not the outgoing edge-end. We study the deterministic problem obtained after this missing vertex datum is supplied. On each edge \(e\), with coordinate \(q\in[0,\ell_e]\), the Hamiltonian is \(H_e(q,p)=p^2/2+V_e(q)\), where \(V\) is continuous on the graph and \(C^2\) on every edge. At each vertex we prescribe an energy-preserving Borel isomorphism from incoming to outgoing nonzero boundary covectors. The resulting phase space is the measurable quotient that identifies each incoming boundary covector with its prescribed outgoing one. After excluding the finitely many energy levels \(V(v)\), the edgewise Hamilton equations and the vertex laws concatenate to a global one-parameter group of bimeasurable transformations. The group preserves energy and the quotient measure induced by the edgewise Liouville measures \(dq\,dp\). The proof uses no smooth symplectic structure on the quotient; the invariance follows from ordinary edgewise Liouville invariance, a uniform no-Zeno estimate on compact regular energy windows, and preservation of the transverse Liouville flux \(r\,dr\) by the speedwise vertex permutations. If the vertex laws are compatible with momentum reversal, then the quotient dynamics is reversible. On regular energy surfaces satisfying the usual regular-value condition, the induced time-parametrization measure is invariant as well.
