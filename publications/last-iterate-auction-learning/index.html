Last-iterate learning in first-price auctions

Why symmetric gradient learning converges to equilibrium even when monotonicity fails.

← All publications

The problem

Can bidders learn an equilibrium by repeatedly adjusting their bids toward higher profit? In a Bayesian first-price auction, each bidder has a private value, and the winner pays its own bid. Knowing the equilibrium bidding strategy does not explain whether learning will reach it.

This project studies symmetric learning: all bidders start with the same bid function and update together. Standard monotonicity and Minty conditions fail in this setting, so convergence needs a different explanation.

What this work shows

  • Subtracting the equilibrium reveals an exact transport-and-damping equation for the bidding error. A comparison principle replaces monotonicity in the convergence argument.
  • Under the stated assumptions on the value distribution and initial bid function, the current bid function converges to equilibrium: exponentially with two bidders and at a sharp polynomial rate with three or more.
  • A fixed-step grid algorithm also has a convergence guarantee under bounded gradient errors in the uniform two-bidder setting. The draft includes a Lean 4 formalization of all numbered results.

The guarantees concern the current bidding strategy, rather than an average over past strategies. Independently evolving, asymmetric bidders remain outside the scope of these results.

    Bichler, M., Hierhager, P., & Hoehener, J. (2026). Last-Iterate Convergence of Gradient Learning in Bayesian First-Price Auctions Beyond Monotonicity.

    Abstract

    We prove last-iterate convergence of symmetric payoff-gradient learning in Bayesian first-price auctions with a continuum of private values, although monotonicity and the Minty condition fail. We use an exactly equivalent procurement representation for the analysis. The key is an exact identity: subtracting the equilibrium turns the bid dynamics into a damped transport equation for the equilibrium error, and a comparison principle for this equation replaces monotonicity. The identity holds exactly when the winner’s surplus is affine in its own price. For every number of bidders \(n\) and every value distribution with a density bounded away from zero and infinity, the dynamics have a unique global solution from every initial bid function with bounded positive slopes, and the sup-norm error decays exponentially for \(n=2\) and as \(t^{-1/(n-2)}\) for \(n\ge3\); both orders are sharp. Nonnegative bids and no overbidding fix the lowest-value bid at zero; without this constraint, it is learned for \(n=2\) and frozen for \(n\ge3\). A fixed-step grid algorithm with gradient errors \(\eta=O(1/N)\) on \(N\) cells converges geometrically to an \(O(\eta)\) neighbourhood of equilibrium in the uniform duopoly; this noise scale is necessary up to a factor of two. All numbered results are formally verified in Lean 4.
